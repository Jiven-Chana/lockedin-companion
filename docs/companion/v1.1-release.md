# LockedIn v1.1: release note and test checklist

**Tag:** `lockedin-v1.1` · **Built from:** [`v1.1-plan.md`](v1.1-plan.md) · **Evidence:** [`v1.1-research.md`](v1.1-research.md) · **Decisions:** [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) V-34 to V-38

v1.1 does two things. Progress becomes the history of what you have learned, with a score, the modules it came from, the gaps that need attention and milestones you keep. The Week page becomes a timetable whose study blocks you can drag.

---

## 1. Run it

```bash
cd ~/PycharmProjects/LockedIn && git checkout main && git pull
nvm use 26 && npm ci
npm run build
LH_DATA_DIR=$PWD/runs/v1 npx next start -p 3000
```

Stop the server that is running first. Your data folder needs no change: the score is worked out from the history that is already there. The phone relay is unchanged, so there is nothing to deploy.

## 2. What changed

### Progress

| Before | Now |
|---|---|
| A list of every week of every module, most of it empty, repeating the Library | Gone (V-38). The Library is where units are listed |
| Three counters | One **learning score**, with "+N this week" and "x of y ideas secured" |
| No history | A chart of the score over the **week, month or term**. Tap or arrow to a point to see what happened then |
| No sense of pace | A dashed **steady-pace line**: a straight line to every idea in your study packs being secured by the end of term |
| No link to modules | **Where it came from**: points and time per module for the range you are looking at |
| "Ideas to revisit" in one order | **Needs attention**: the module you have done least in two weeks, ideas that are fading, and modules with no material, each with one button. Ideas to revisit lists that module first |
| Nothing to keep | **Fifteen milestones** in three groups (learning, discipline, well-rounded). Once earned, one is never taken away |

How the score is earned (the numbers are `SCORE_RULES` in `policy.ts`):

| What you do | Points |
|---|---|
| A right answer | 10, 15 or 20 by difficulty |
| An idea secured | +20 |
| An idea brought back from "to revisit" | +25 |
| A study block started | +5 |
| A wrong answer or "I don't know" | 0, never a loss |
| An idea not looked at for 21 days | −5 until you get it back |

There are no levels, no streaks and no leaderboard (V-35). The gap to the steady-pace line is never printed as a number and nothing says "behind".

The two things you saw on the old page, checked in your data folder: the page listed every timetable lecture whether or not it had material (59 of its 67 rows were empty), and the weeks looked doubled because your chapters (weeks 1 to 7) sat beside timetable lectures that are also numbered 1 to 7. The timetable's numbers are not teaching weeks: "week 1" is the first week the calendar import could see. Both went with the list. Those week numbers are hidden on Progress, not renumbered: that is still open.

### Week

- **A timetable on wide screens:** days across, hours down. Lectures, classes and routine commitments sit at their real times and don't move.
- **Drag a study block** to any free time or day. It snaps to 15 minutes.
- **It can't land on** a lecture, a commitment, another block or quiet hours. It goes back and a line says why. The server checks the same rule.
- **Tap a block** for its options at the bottom of the screen: keep, move by typing, remove, and **which module it is for**. "Free study" is a choice.
- **Keyboard:** Tab to a block, arrow keys to move it (15 minutes, or a day), Enter to drop, Escape to put it back.
- **Blocks carry a module.** "Plan my week" gives each new block the module of the unit it studies. Blocks made before v1.1 that had no unit read "Free study" until you give them a module.
- **Narrow screens** keep the list of days, where there is no room to drag.

Not in v1.1: stretching a block by its edge, and adding a block by clicking an empty slot.

## 3. Test checklist

### Progress
1. Open **Progress**. You see one score, "+N this week", and a chart. No list of weeks.
2. Press **Week**, **Month**, **Term**. The chart and "Where it came from" change with the range.
3. Hover or tap a point. The line under the chart names the day or week, the score, what you got right, and any idea secured or brought back.
4. Find a diamond on the chart. The point under it names the milestone.
5. **Needs attention** names a module you have done little on, or says nothing needs attention. Press its button: it starts a session on that module, or opens Library for a module with no material.
6. Do a session and get a question right. Come back to Progress: the score has gone up by at least 10.
7. **Milestones** shows what you have earned with its date, and how to earn the rest.

### Week
8. Open **Week** on your laptop. You see a timetable, with your lectures at their times.
9. Drag a study block to an empty slot the same day. It stays there after a reload.
10. Drag it to another day. Same.
11. Drag it onto a lecture. It turns to the warning colour, goes back when you let go, and the line under the grid says "That overlaps a lecture or class."
12. Drag it past 22:00 (or your own quiet hours). It goes back.
13. Tap a block. Its options appear at the bottom of the screen. Change **Module**: the block changes colour and name.
14. Tab to a block, press ↓ twice and Enter. It moves 30 minutes.
15. Press **Plan my week**. New proposals are dashed and carry a module.
16. Make the window narrow (or open it on a phone-sized screen). The list of days replaces the grid.

## 4. How it was checked

- **Rules first:** 68 table-driven tests for the score, the milestones, attention, block modules and where a block may go (`tests/companion-score.test.ts`, `companion-milestones.test.ts`, `companion-progress.test.ts`). `npm run companion:check`: 705 pass. `npm test`: 6,018 pass, none fail. Typecheck and lint are clean.
- **Against real history:** the score was replayed on a copy of Jiven's own data folder (155 points, 4 of 63 ideas secured, 4 milestones) and on a copy with 50 more answers added (1,235 points, 31 of 63, 2 fading, 10 milestones).
- **In a browser:** the built pages were opened in Chrome and driven with a real mouse and keyboard. A block dropped on a lecture was refused and unchanged on the server; a block was moved within a day, across days and by keyboard, and each move was read back from the server; the module was changed from the options; the list replaced the grid at 420 px. No page errors.
- **Not checked:** dragging with a finger on a touch screen, and a full term of real history.
