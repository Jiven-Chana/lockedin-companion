# LockedIn v2.0: smooth, bespoke, meaningful

**Status:** direction, 24 Nov 2026. Work on this starts **after v1.0 is stable** (`v1.0-issues.md`). This is not an approved plan. Each section ends with the questions to settle before it becomes one.

**In one line:** v0.1 built the pieces, and v1.0 makes them work. v2.0 makes LockedIn feel like **one calm path from "lecture just ended" to "I know this"**, with nothing a student has to figure out, and every screen earning its place.

**What it's built on:**
- the team's own design work: storyboards T14, sketches T10 and their scores, the lo-fi prototype v12 with its change log, the UI research for the lo-fi, the onboarding spike and its walkthrough (AE2), and `design-log.md`;
- Jiven's testing of v0.1 on 23–24 Nov.

Lo-fi user testing (T18) runs on 25 Nov. Its results feed into this doc, and nothing here should override them.

---

## 1. Where we drifted from our own design

The team's design work already describes a smoother product than the one v0.1 built. The fastest route to v2.0 is to come back to it.

| The design said | v0.1 did | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Value before setup:** one question before any account; setup is 3 taps, under a minute | Step 1 is "Connect your AI" with three model pickers and thinking levels | 10 Nov decision; SB4; AE2-1, AE2-P14 |
| **No model picker in setup:** "Never in the start flow"; models sit two levels down under Advanced | Model slots are on onboarding step 1 and at the top of Me | Anti-persona Dev (A-2): "we (the team) are Dev" |
| **Material arrives on its own:** "drop a PDF … is setup, and setup kills starts" | A full upload flow: files → units → weeks → save → start each pack by hand | S-62 (changed by research); lo-fi has no upload screen |
| **Every setup answer visibly changes something** | Most setup answers change nothing you can see | AFF-G; AE2-P10 (the theme picker was "the only delight") |
| **No blank page:** every tap opens on a question with a goal | Library, lecture pages and Progress are lists to browse | R-4 |
| **Show the source slide** | Raw PDF text, or "No material" (v1.0 S-1, S-2) | INT-01-g, INT-02-i |
| **Settings:** four lines plus "Advanced ▸" | Ten sections on Me | Lo-fi v12 settings |
| **Done screens name what comes back Friday;** no score | Mostly kept, but Progress shows counts | 23 Nov decision; R-6 |
| **Library, Progress and Me** | Never designed in the lo-fi (greyed placeholders); v0.1 invented them | Lo-fi v12 |

The last row matters most: **the screens that feel cluttered are the ones nobody designed.** v2.0 designs them, starting from the same rules.

## 2. The rules v2.0 keeps (from the design work)

1. **Value before setup.** A question first, setup after, and pairing after that (SB4).
2. **The nudge is the first question.** One a day, three buttons, with "why now" (R-1, R-3, R-7).
3. **The phone starts, the laptop teaches** (R-8).
4. **Tap, don't type.** Recognition over recall, and typing only where it adds something (the "why" box).
5. **No blank page.** Every screen opens on the next step, with a goal (R-4).
6. **Every setup answer shows its effect straight away** (AFF-G).
7. **Choices per session, not a profile.** Guided versus open, and show-first versus question-first, are picked in the moment (S-20, S-66).
8. **No guilt.** No streaks, no percentages, no overdue backlog: "fade, not debt" (R-5, R-6).
9. **The tiniest first step:** the best-evidenced sketch (S-67, scored 8 of 10).
10. **Show the source, label the AI.**
11. **Dev is the anti-persona.** Anything technical lives under Advanced.

## 3. Getting started: two minutes to value

**Today:** connect your AI (models, thinking levels), then timetable, then modules, then a first taste. That's four screens before the first question, and the first is the most technical.

**v2.0 flow (SB4, updated):**

1. **One question, no account.** "Try one question from a sample HCI lecture." The bundled first taste already has hand-written questions, so it runs on the scripted engine and **needs no AI at all**. After a right answer: "Took 30 seconds. Want this after every lecture?"
2. **Your timetable.** One box, with "Where do I find my link?" (done 24 Nov). The modules appear ticked as soon as the link is pasted. Seeing your own modules appear is the "it changed something" moment.
3. **Your AI, in one button.** "Sign in with ChatGPT" (or "I have an API key"). Models are chosen automatically and never shown. This comes third because it's the first point where the AI is needed: writing questions from *your* slides.
4. **Your week**, built from those answers (lo-fi): lectures greyed, two suggested blocks, and "because you have energy in the afternoon". **Looks good** finishes setup.
5. **Later, never in the start flow:** your phone ("Get questions after lectures, even with the laptop shut"), offered on Today after the first real session.

**Target:** first question under 60 seconds; own timetable and AI connected under 3 minutes; no typing except the link.

**To settle:**
- Does the energy question stay in the week step, or move into the routine chat?
- What exactly appears if ChatGPT sign-in fails?

## 4. Your courses (replacing "Library")

**Today:** a module list, an upload dialog, units with weeks, a pack button per unit, and four tabs per lecture.

**v2.0:** your material comes in once and sorts itself.

- **One drop, everything.** "Drop all your slides and notes for this term." Any number of files, or a whole folder, in any shape: per lecture, per week, a semester of notes.
- **Sorted for you.** The AI matches each file to a module and to lectures **using the timetable**, so there are no weeks to type.
  - Notes are recognised as notes and split by chapter or idea.
  - Anything unsure is shown once, as "Is this Game Theory week 3?" (Yes / Change).
- **Prepared in the background,** one at a time (v1.0 T-4). Each lecture shows one line: *ready*, *preparing*, or *needs you*. No pack button, no stages to read.
- **A lecture opens on its map:** the ideas as a picture, coloured by how well you know each one, with one main button, **Learn this** or **Practise this** (section 5). Slides, explainers and revisits are reached *from* the map, not as four tabs.
- **No "Week 0".** If a week truly can't be found, the lecture says "week not set", with a one-tap fix.

**To settle:**
- Folder drop, or Canvas import later (needs Canvas access; out of scope until decided)?
- How much sorting does a student check versus trust?

## 5. Two ways to study, and making them bespoke

The engine already runs the team's learning loop: **probe → learn → predict → check** (`research/literature-learning-loop.md`, LIT-24 to LIT-31). v0.1 hides it behind one quiz screen. v2.0 makes the difference visible and meaningful.

### Learn (teaching): for a lecture you haven't got yet
- **Probe:** a quick look at what you already know, *framed as finding your starting point, never as a test*. AE2-9 warns that probing "read as being tested before being taught". Then the map shows: "you've got 3 of 7 ideas already".
- **Per idea:**
  - **learn** one step (a short explainer with its diagram and the source slide);
  - **predict** what comes next (a guess, no penalty: LIT-26, LIT-30);
  - **check** with one question (LIT-24, LIT-29).
- **The map fills in as you go.** That's the progress, and no separate screen is needed.
- **Long lectures (80+ slides) are taught idea by idea,** a few at a time, never page by page (v1.0 T-3).

### Practise (quizzes): for revision
- **Five quick questions from your own question bank,** mixed across ideas.
- Weighted towards **what you got wrong or weren't sure of** (confidence-weighted, LIT-27). This is what "Ready to revisit" already collects.
- **The phone's question is a practice question.** "4 more on your laptop" continues the practice.

### Bespoke: what to study next is chosen for you, with a reason
- **Today offers one thing,** for example: "GT · Nash equilibrium: learn it (you haven't yet), 12 min". It's picked from your timetable, your pace (V-18: keep up first, a little catch-up), and what's fading.
- **"Why this"** is always one line, in the "why now" style.
- **Choices are per session:** show me first or question first, Learn or Practise (S-66, S-20). No learner profile is built up front. The onboarding spike's archetypes were rejected *up front* (AE2-P14); an optional "how you learn" view could come later (AE2-P16: it was "buy-in", but "too much").

**To settle:**
- Is "Learn" and "Practise" the right wording?
- Does the probe always run, or only on a lecture's first session?
- How visible should the loop's steps be: named on screen, or only felt?

## 6. Slides and visuals

**Today:** the slide panel shows text pulled out of the PDF (garbled maths, one label per line). Mermaid diagrams are decent but generic.

### The source slide: show the real page
Render the actual PDF page as an image beside the question (pdf.js), at the page the explanation cites. That's honest, readable and trusted (INT-01-g). The extracted text stays behind the scenes, for search and for the AI.

### Visual summaries: the trade-off

| Approach | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| **Mermaid** (today) | The AI writes it reliably; flows, cycles, mind maps; safe | Looks generic; little control over layout; limited motion |
| **AI-written SVG** | Anything is possible; can be beautiful | Often broken or overlapping; inconsistent style between diagrams; a security risk (SVG can carry scripts) unless cleaned; hard to animate meaningfully |
| **Structured visuals (recommended)** | The AI only chooses a *kind* and fills in data: a cycle, a step sequence, a comparison, a 2×2 or payoff matrix, a timeline, a labelled diagram of parts. Our own components draw it in LockedIn's style, with theme colours, calm motion that reveals step by step, and KaTeX for maths. Always valid, always on-brand, animatable, accessible (a text version comes for free) | We build and maintain the component set; anything outside the set needs a fallback |
| **Hand-drawn style** (rough.js on top of the above) | Warm and friendly (the lo-fi's hand-drawn feel); less "corporate diagram" | A matter of taste; can look less precise for maths-heavy subjects |
| **KaTeX** (keep) | Proper maths | Formulas only |

**Proposed:** structured visuals as the main way to explain an idea, with a small, well-made set of kinds, chosen per idea by the AI. Mermaid stays as the fallback for anything that doesn't fit a kind. Real slide pages appear wherever the source matters.

Subject-specific kinds pay off quickly. For example, a **payoff matrix** with highlighted best responses (Game Theory), a **pipeline** (Neural Computation), and **steps with a worked example** (maths).

### Progress as a picture
- **Per lecture:** the concept map, coloured *solid / shaky / to revisit / not yet* (it exists; make it the main view).
- **Per module:** a path of lectures showing where you are this week. The team sketched this as a guided roadmap (S-18), a topic map (S-21) and a metro map (S-54). S-21 and S-54 were *contested*, so test one before committing.
- **No counts or percentages** (R-6). The picture *is* the progress.

**To settle:**
- Is the structured-visuals approach agreed?
- Which first set of kinds?
- Hand-drawn style or clean?
- Which module-level picture to test?

## 7. Today: one next step

**Today (v0.1):** a hero card, starter cards, a lecture list, revisit, this week's counts, the next block, and "Make it yours".

**v2.0 (from the lo-fi):** one line of context and one action.
- "HCI lecture 11:00–13:00 · free until 15:00 · next block Thu 14:00".
- **One card:** the next thing to do (section 5), with its reason.
- **Only when relevant:** the phone hand-off card ("Question 1 answered on your phone ✓"), or the Sunday check-in.
- Everything else is one tap away, not on the home screen.

## 9. Phone: install that just works

**Today:** the QR code opens a page with one sentence about the Home Screen (v1.0 P-1).

**v2.0:**
- **A guided install:** pictures of the Share button and "Add to Home Screen", a live check, then "✓ Installed. Allow notifications".
- **A sample question you can answer straight away** in the browser, so the page is never empty.
- **The question in the notification itself:** answer buttons on the lock screen where the phone allows it (Android). On iPhone, the tap opens straight onto the question.
- **Pairing after the first real session,** never during setup (SB4).
- **If a guided web install still fails in testing,** consider a thin native wrapper later. That's out of scope until T4 results are in.

## 10. Me: four lines and "Advanced"

From lo-fi v12:
- **Your AI:** "Connected to ChatGPT ✓" or "Connect".
- **Phone:** paired / pair.
- **Notifications:** nudges per day (0 / 1 / 2) and quiet hours.
- **Appearance:** theme, colour, text size, motion. This is the delight moment (AE2-P10), so keep it live and visible.
- **What LockedIn uses:** timetable yes, slides yes, location never; and "Delete all my data".
- **Advanced ▸:** models, thinking levels, voice key, calendar link details, study-block settings, routine. Dev's corner.

## 11. Clutter to remove

- Model slots and thinking levels anywhere outside Advanced.
- The stage-by-stage pack progress ("read → map → bank → explainers"). One line instead.
- Four tabs per lecture: the map leads; the rest is reached from it.
- Today's starter cards and stat counts.
- Week numbers wherever they mean nothing to the student.
- Any screen that can't answer "what's the next step here?".

---

## 12. How we'll know it's smoother

- **Time to the first question** (sample): under 60 s. **To your own lecture's first question:** under 3 minutes after setup.
- **Setup taps:** at most 5, with no typing except the calendar link.
- **Screens a student needs** for "lecture ended → answered → handed off": three (notification, phone result, laptop session).
- **Testers find every action without help.** This is T18/T23-style tasks, with the T22 heuristics (visibility of status, user control, recognition over recall).
- **No session ends with "something got stuck".**
