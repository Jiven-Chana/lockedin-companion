# LockedIn v2.0: smooth, bespoke, meaningful

**Status:** direction, rewritten 24 Nov 2026 (evening) after v1.1 shipped. This is **not yet an approved plan.** Section 2 records what Jiven has decided (V-39 to V-51); section 17 lists what is still to settle. The build plan follows as `v2.0-plan.md` once those are answered.

**In one line:** v0.1 built the pieces, v1.0 made them work and v1.1 made progress mean something. v2.0 makes LockedIn **one calm path from "lecture just ended" to "I know this"**, shaped like the lo-fi the team designed and tested, with every screen traceable to the research.

**What this is built from:**
- **The team's design work** (`kayzisland/hcidesignchallenge`, read at `origin/main`, commit `d09407a`): lo-fi v13 and its change log, KAYZ's sketch, the T14 storyboards, the T10 sketches and scores, T20 behaviour change, the device-split QOC v2.1, the findings register, the design log and the T38 feature chains.
- **The build at `lockedin-v1.1`,** audited screen by screen against the lo-fi on 24 Nov (section 3).
- **Jiven's testing** of v0.1, v1.0 and v1.1 on his own six modules.

**Two results are still to come and outrank this doc:** the lo-fi test (T18, 25 Nov) and the hi-fi test (T23). Where a section depends on one, it says so.

## 1. The shape of v2.0

LockedIn has two devices and, from v2.0, two ways to study. Each device has one job.

| | **Phone** | **Laptop** |
|---|---|---|
| **Its job** | Get you started | Teach you |
| **What you do there** | Answer one question, the moment a lecture ends, and keep practising if you want to. See your week. Check in on Sunday | **Teach:** learn what you haven't got yet. **Practise:** quick questions on what you have covered |
| **Why** | It reaches you in the free gap (Q-8). It is also the distraction, so learning doesn't run there (R-8; AFF-M; LIT-6) | Place commits people (AFF-J), and the engine already teaches well there (AE2-10) |

The phone's question is a Practise question. After it, the student chooses: **"4 more on your laptop"**, **"Keep going here"**, or **"Done for now"** (V-46). Teach never runs on the phone: it needs the slide, room to say what you want, and more than a free minute.

**How this sits with the team's rule.** The device split was a working choice, and the QOC (v2.1) wrote down in advance what would overturn it: the laptop-only hand-off stays only if at least 3 of 5 testers in T18 pick the laptop; anything else moves to "the student chooses where to carry on" (option O5). Jiven has chosen O5 for Practise ahead of that result. So T18 now tests a choice already made, and its answer goes in the deck either way. It is a step away from R-8's strict reading ("learning sessions do not run on the phone"), kept for Teach.

## 4. Two ways to study: Teach and Practise (V-40)

Today there is one session screen. Underneath, it is either the AI teaching live or a fixed list of questions, and the student is never told which. The only visible difference is the words "· scripted questions". v2.0 makes them two things a student chooses between, each good at one job.

| | **Teach** | **Practise** |
|---|---|---|
| **For** | An idea you haven't got yet | Keeping what you have covered |
| **Feels like** | Being taught, one idea at a time | A quick round |
| **The loop** | Probe, learn, predict, check: the structure underneath, loose on top | Answer, see why, next |
| **Questions** | Written live by the AI for you; any form; reads your reasoning | From your study pack's question bank; three options |
| **Covers** | What you ask for: one section, a lecture, or a whole week | A whole module: mixed ideas, mixed difficulty |
| **Speed** | Seconds per turn while the AI thinks | Instant |
| **Length** | As long as the block; picked up later if unfinished | 5 questions, about 3 minutes; "3 more" at the end |
| **Runs on** | Laptop only | Laptop or phone (V-46). The phone's question is its first question |
| **Needs the AI** | Yes | No. It works with no AI connected |

### Teach (V-47)
The engine already runs the team's loop (`research/literature-learning-loop.md`, LIT-24 to LIT-31): probe, learn, predict, check. Today it is hidden behind one quiz screen, and the probe was switched off because the engine's own "asked a fair few questions and isn't very kind to the user" (Jiven). v2.0 keeps the loop as the structure underneath and makes the session loose on top.

**How a Teach session goes**
1. **You say what you want.** Pick a lecture, pick "this week", or type or say it: "the simplex method, I've seen it but can't do the pivots". You also say where you think you are.
2. **The probe is sized to that, and you can skip it.**
   - A whole week in one session: a few high-level questions across it.
   - One section: a couple of pointed ones on that section.
   - It is "have a guess", never marked wrong, and never a gap (LIT-26, LIT-30). "Skip, just teach me" is always there, because probing before teaching "read as being tested" (AE2-9, X-4).
3. **The plan appears as a picture:** the ideas this session will cover, in order, with what you already have marked. It is the session's map and its progress bar.
4. **Each idea is taught through the loop,** loosely: learn it (explainer, picture, the real slide), predict what comes next, check it with one question. Along the way you can ask for a hint, a worked example of a similar question, the same thing explained another way, or just ask a question.
5. **Out of time is fine.** The session is saved where it stopped, with its plan, and is offered again in your next study block on that topic. Nothing is lost and nothing is marked missed (R-5; AFF-B; AE2-P12).

**What stays the student's choice every time:** what to learn, how much, show-me-first or question-first, and whether to be probed (AFF-N: "a choice per visit, never a profile").

**What LockedIn learns about you (V-48)**
- **A short conversation** about how you like to learn: what helps, what puts you off, how much you like to be questioned. A few taps and a line or two, skippable.
- **It shapes the loop:** how much probing, examples before questions or after, how long an explanation runs, how often to check.
- **It keeps growing from what you do,** not only from what you said: what you skip, what you ask for, where you get stuck.
- **You can see it and change it.** A "How I learn" card with what LockedIn thinks, each line editable. The onboarding spike's override sliders were the part testers valued (AE2-P16).

**The tension, on the record.** The group decided against profile-first onboarding on 10 Nov, after a 15-question onboarding "was perceived as roughly 60 questions" (AE2-P14), and on 16 Nov that "feature toggles live in Settings and aren't chosen by an LLM". AFF-N makes teaching mode a per-session choice. V-48 keeps to the spirit of all three if it holds to four rules: **short** (minutes, not a questionnaire), **skippable**, **every answer changes something you can see at once** (AFF-G), and **never locking a choice** the student can make per session. It is still a departure, and is owed a design-log entry and a T23 question.

**Keeping the AI's memory small.** Teaching across six modules for a term means the AI can't re-read everything each time, and long sessions are where it trips. v2.0 gives it three short notes instead of a pile: how this student learns (one page), where they are in this module (one page per module), and this session's plan. *How the engine handles memory today is being checked; this paragraph is a direction until then.*

### Practise
- **Mixed on purpose.** Questions are drawn across a module's ideas, not one lecture in order. Mixing topics helps retention more than blocking them (Brunmair & Richter 2019, in `v1.1-research.md`; **the team's register has nothing on interleaving, so this needs a `LIT` id**).
- **Mixed difficulty.** Every bank question is already marked recall, understand or apply. A round has some of each, easiest first, so the first step feels good (LIT-13, LIT-14).
- **Weighted to your gaps.** Ideas you missed, or were confidently wrong about, come up more (LIT-27; INT-01-d). Ideas that are fading come next.
- **Never the same question twice running,** and not the same five every time. Today a Practise-style session always takes the first five questions in the bank.
- **The slide is beside every question,** and the explanation cites it.
- **Fast:** pick an answer, tap how sure you are (Guess, Unsure, Fairly sure, Certain), see why. No reason box (V-51). How sure you were is what separates a lucky right answer from a secure one, and a confident error from a guess (LIT-27).

### Why two modes, and not something else
| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| One session that adapts by itself (today) | The student can't tell teaching from testing, and X-4 shows the same probing is welcomed in one frame and resented in the other |
| A fixed profile set at onboarding ("I like quizzes") | Rejected by the group: mode is "a choice per visit, never a profile" (AFF-N; 10 Nov). V-48 is a profile that informs and can be overridden each session, not one that locks |
| Practise only | Leaves out what the engine is for. The AI taught well (AE2-10) and a guarded tutor beats an unguarded one (LIT-25, LIT-28) |
| Teach only | Too slow for a free gap, and it can't run on the phone (R-8) |

### What has to change in the build
- Session kinds today are `post-lecture`, `missed-lecture`, `retest`, `try-now` and `block`. None is a mode. Mode becomes its own field.
- The bank picker takes the first N questions by difficulty. It needs the mixing rule above, as a pure rule with tests.
- Each question already carries `check` or `predict`; the screen ignores it.
- A bank question knows its slides; the session doesn't pass them on.

## 5. Gaps by idea (V-39), replacing "Ideas to revisit"

**The problem.** "Ideas to revisit" is a flat list of every question missed, grouped by lecture. It only grows. A long list of things you got wrong is a backlog, and a backlog is what nearly made INT-01 abandon his flashcard app (2,000 lapsed cards; the system "can't be vindictive").

**The design.** Three levels, and you only ever see the top one until you ask.

```
IDEAS TO REVISIT

Game Theory                       3 ideas
 ▸ Dual simplex method           2 missed
 ▾ Nash equilibrium              3 missed
     · Which strategy pair is...   Slide 14
     · Why is (B, R) not an...     Slide 15
     · A game has two pure...      Slide 17
     [ Practise this idea: 5 new questions ]
 ▸ Dominated strategies          1 missed

Neural Computation                1 idea
 ▸ Sampling from a generator     4 missed
```

1. **Module, then idea.** An idea is one of the 2 to 10 ideas each study pack already names; every bank question is tagged with one. Only ideas with a gap are listed. The module that most needs attention is first (V-36).
2. **Open an idea** to see the questions you missed on it. Open a question to see what you chose, the right answer, why, and the slide.
3. **"Practise this idea"** writes new questions aimed at the gap.

### New questions, written around the gap
- **What the AI is given:** the idea and its slides, the questions you missed, **what you chose and the reason you typed,** and how sure you were. A confident wrong answer is the most useful thing a session produces (LIT-27), and feedback must address "the reasoning leading up to the mistake".
- **What it writes:** a short round on that idea only, in new words, at more than one difficulty, each citing its slide.
- **Guardrails (LIT-25; Sam, the anti-persona):** the answer is never shown before you commit. "Show me first" gives a worked example of a *similar* question.
- **Trust (X-9, unresolved; synthesis change 6):** every AI-written question is labelled as written by AI and links to its source slide. Half the interviewees distrust AI.
- **Kept, not thrown away:** new questions join the idea's bank, so Practise can use them later.
- **No AI connected:** the round uses unused bank questions on the same idea.

### How a gap closes, and how the list stays short
- **A right answer anywhere counts,** not only inside a revisit session.
- **An idea leaves the list after two right answers on different questions about it, on two different days,** each marked "Fairly sure" or "Certain" (V-43). Spacing and repetition are what the evidence supports (LIT-17, LIT-24), and one lucky answer can't hide a gap.
- **Fade, not debt.** Nothing is dated, nothing is "overdue", nothing turns red (R-6; T20 section 6; S-22: "5 chosen items, not a backlog"; S-58). "3 missed" names what is there; it never names how late you are.
- **Friday's review** in the lo-fi becomes "comes back in your next Practise".

### What has to change in the build
- **What you chose is not recorded** for laptop answers. It has to be, with the typed reason.
- **A miss is stored per question per session,** and is cleared only in a revisit session at 70% confidence or more. It becomes per idea.
- **Question ids are positional.** Re-making a study pack reuses the same ids for different questions. Ids must stay stable before questions can be added.
- **There is no way today to write questions for one idea.** The AI writes whole packs, or writes live inside a Teach session without saving.
- The API doesn't send the idea with a miss; it will.

### Evidence, and what it was chosen over
| | |
|---|---|
| **For** | Students want the weak spot directly (INT-01-d, INT-02-e, INT-05-e, INT-06-f). Errors repeat and resurfacing them helps (AFF-E; AE-2, AE-6, AE-8). Testing with feedback, repeated, is what works (LIT-16, LIT-17, LIT-24). Students keep tools that write the questions for them (T-9) |
| **Sketches** | S-02 chunk map ("measure, then target what was missed"), S-03 ("Still fuzzy: Gulfs"), S-22 re-entry |
| **Chosen over** | A flat list (today; grows without limit). By chapter (too coarse to aim questions). Topics named by the AI across a module (a new AI step with no evidence). A due-date queue like Anki's (INT-01-i) |
| **Limits** | AFF-E is single-method (C-6). INT-01's interface ideas came from co-design and are "one participant's suggestion". **Nothing in the research tests whether AI-written questions are good enough,** and nothing covers varying a question. T23 should ask |

## 6. Getting started (V-45)

**Today:** connect your AI (three model pickers, thinking levels), then timetable, then modules, then a sample lecture. The first screen is the most technical one, and most of what LockedIn needs to know about the student is asked later or never.

**v2.0:** five short steps that tell LockedIn who you are, then straight into your own material.

```
1  Sign in with ChatGPT         one button
2  Your timetable               paste the link
                                → your modules appear
3  Your slides and notes        drop them all in
                                → "Preparing Game Theory..."
4  Your week                    when do you have energy?
                                → blocks, each with a reason
                                → "Looks good"
5  Your phone                   scan, type the code

→  "Teach me: Nash equilibrium"  (your own lecture)
```

- **One main action per step.** No model pickers: models are chosen for you and live under Advanced (anti-persona Dev, A-2).
- **Every step except sign-in can be skipped** with "Later".
- **Every answer changes something you can see** (AFF-G; AE2-P10): modules appear as the link is pasted, units appear as files land, blocks move when you pick your energy.
- **Sign-in is first for a reason:** slides can only be prepared once the AI is connected. From step 3 the study packs are written in the background, so the first Teach session is ready when setup ends.
- **How you learn (V-48)** is asked as part of that first Teach session, not as a sixth setup step. *Where exactly is to settle (section 17).*

### Why not the lo-fi's "one question first"
The lo-fi's version A, the 10 Nov group decision and storyboard 4 all put one sample question before any setup, because setup cost is what lost the autoethnographer (AE2-1: six steps before any value; AE2-P14).

The build already left that order on 23 Nov, when V-3 put the AI sign-in on screen 1 with a sample lecture straight after. V-45 keeps the sign-in first and replaces the sample with the student's own material.

**Jiven's reason for departing:** a sample question is hard-coded, so it can't be about the student. "We need to understand the user first"; the first taste should be their own lecture, taught properly. The lo-fi's own change log half agrees: it had to relabel the first question "a sample question" because it was not the student's lecture (R-7, AFF-H).

**What that costs, and how v2.0 pays for it:**
- **Setup now comes before value,** which the research warns against. So setup has to be short: five steps, one action each, skippable, with something visible changing at each. **Target: under 3 minutes to "Looks good", and no typing except the timetable link.**
- **Pairing moves into setup,** against UI research Q3 ("pairing once, after the first question"). It is the last step and can be skipped.
- **T18 tests exactly this tomorrow** (task 3: question first against ChatGPT first). If testers clearly prefer question-first, that result and this decision both go in the deck, with the reasoning. That is the honest version of rubric criterion 8.

## 7. Today: one next step

**Today (v1.1):** a hero card, starter cards, a lecture list, revisit, counts, the next block.

**v2.0 (from the lo-fi):** one line of context and one action.
- "HCI lecture 11:00–13:00 · free until 15:00 · next block Thu 14:00".

- **"Why this"** is always one line, in the "why now" style (R-7).

- Everything else is one tap away.

No blank page: every screen opens on the next step, with a goal (R-4).

## 8. Your courses (replacing "Library")

**Today:** a module list, an upload dialog, units with weeks, a pack button per unit, and four tabs per lecture.

**v2.0:** your material comes in once and sorts itself.
- **One drop, everything.** Any number of files, in any shape: per lecture, per week, a term of notes.
- **Sorted for you,** using the timetable. Anything unsure is asked once: "Is this Game Theory week 3?"
- **A chapter can span several weeks.** Today a unit has one week number, and Jiven's seven chapters cover a whole term.
- **Week numbers match the university's.** Today the timetable counts from the first week the import could see (V-38 hides them; this fixes them).
- **Prepared in the background,** one line per unit: *ready*, *preparing*, *needs you*. No pack button, no stages to read.
- **A lecture opens on its map:** the ideas as a picture drawn by LockedIn (V-33), coloured by how well you know each, with two buttons, **Learn this** and **Practise this**. Slides, explainers and gaps are reached from the map, not as four tabs.

## 9. The phone

**What it is in v2.0:** the place a session starts. One question after a lecture, your week, and the Sunday check-in. Pairing by a six-digit code (V-32) is confirmed on a real iPhone and stays.

**What v2.0 adds:**
- **"Keep going here"** after the first question (V-46): the rest of the Practise round on the phone, one at a time, with "Another one" and "Save the rest for later", as in the lo-fi's on-the-move screen.
- "Why now" in the notification itself.
- "Saved. The other 4 wait for your next free gap. Nothing is overdue."
- The result of question 1 on the laptop's hand-off card.
- A block notification that names the first step and opens the block on the laptop (V-44).
- "Next free gap" when moving a block.
- Answer buttons on the notification, if T18 prefers them to tap-to-open.

### App blocking is left out (V-41)
- **A real block can't be built here.** The phone app is a web app, and a web app can't see or stop another app opening. On an iPhone, blocking needs a native app using Apple's Screen Time frameworks (UI research Q2). The lo-fi says so on its own shield: "LockedIn can't really block apps. This is a reminder you chose."
- **The evidence is split.** Five of six interviewees named the phone unprompted (AFF-M), yet none of the five asked endorsed a lock (X-8, unresolved). The group never logged a decision on the lock brief.
- **What stays true for the deck:** app blocking is one of the four core features (23 Nov) and its chain ends at the lo-fi. The hi-fi link is "not built, and why". That is an honest limitation, not a gap to hide (rubric criteria 3 and 8).
- **What the build keeps:** the Off / Gentle / Firm rules already exist on the server, unused. A native app (v3.0 or later) would use them.

## 13. Motion and feel

**The research says nothing about animation.** No finding, sketch or storyboard specifies motion, sound or transitions for LockedIn; the only haptic is "one buzz" when a break ends. So v2.0 takes its rules from findings that are nearby, and says plainly that these are design judgement until T23.

| Rule | From |
|---|---|
| **Motion marks a change you caused.** An answer, a block moved, an idea secured | AFF-G: a question that changes something you can see earns the next one |
| **Show that something is happening** during any wait | AFF-H; AE2-2 |
| **A moved thing stays marked** after the motion ends | Change blindness (Wk 4); S-16 |
| **Calm by default, off on request.** The system's reduced-motion setting is always obeyed | S-66; Q-12 (no separate neurodivergent mode) |
| **Nothing moves to bring you back.** No pulsing badges, no countdowns | R-6; T20 "deliberately absent" |

**Moments that get motion in v2.0** (none of these exist today):
- the question changing to the next one;
- an idea on the map changing state when you secure it;
- the hand-off arriving from the phone;
- a block settling where you dropped it, and snapping back when it can't go there;
- a gap leaving the list;
- the done screen;
- a milestone being earned.

Today's tokens (180, 260 and 300 ms) and the Calm / Full / Off setting stay.
