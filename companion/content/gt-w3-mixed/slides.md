## Slide 1 — Mixed strategies

- Week 3 of Game Theory
- When no pure-strategy equilibrium exists

## Slide 2 — Matching Pennies

- Two players each show Heads or Tails; one wins if they match, the other if they differ
- No pure-strategy profile is a Nash equilibrium: someone always wants to switch

## Slide 3 — Mixing

- A mixed strategy is a probability distribution over pure strategies
- Example: play Heads with probability 1/2 and Tails with probability 1/2

## Slide 4 — The indifference principle

- In a mixed equilibrium, each player mixes so that the other player is indifferent between their pure strategies
- If the other player were not indifferent, they would switch to the better one

## Slide 5 — Nash's theorem

- Every finite game has at least one Nash equilibrium, possibly in mixed strategies (Nash, 1950)
- In Matching Pennies, the unique equilibrium is both players mixing 1/2–1/2
