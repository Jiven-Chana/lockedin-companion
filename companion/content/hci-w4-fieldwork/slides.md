## Slide 1 — Fieldwork and ethnography

- Week 4 of Human-Computer Interaction
- Studying people where the activity actually happens

## Slide 2 — Why go into the field

- What people say they do and what they actually do often differ
- Context shapes behaviour: interruptions, workarounds, other people, the physical setting
- Lab studies and surveys miss much of this

## Slide 3 — What ethnography is

- A research approach from anthropology: immersion in a setting over time to understand it from the participants' point of view
- In HCI it is usually shorter and more focused ("rapid ethnography")
- The aim is understanding, not measuring

## Slide 4 — Participant observation

- The researcher takes part in the activity while observing it
- Ranges from passive observer to full participant
- Trade-off: closer participation gives insider understanding but less detachment

## Slide 5 — Contextual inquiry

- An interview held in the user's own context while they do their real work
- Master–apprentice model: the user is the expert, the researcher learns by watching and asking
- Four principles: context, partnership, interpretation, focus

## Slide 6 — Recording what you see

- Field notes: written as soon as possible, separating what was observed from what you think it means
- Photos, audio and artefacts (with consent)
- "Thick description": enough detail and context that a reader understands what the behaviour means to the people involved

## Slide 7 — The observer effect

- People behave differently when they know they are being watched (the Hawthorne effect)
- Mitigations: spend longer in the field so people get used to you; triangulate with other data

## Slide 8 — Triangulation

- Using more than one data source, method or researcher to check a finding
- For example: what participants say in interviews, what you observe, and usage logs
- A finding supported by several sources is more trustworthy

## Slide 9 — Ethics in the field

- Informed consent: participants understand what you collect and why, and can withdraw
- Anonymity and secure storage of notes and recordings
- Take extra care in shared or public spaces where bystanders did not consent

## Slide 10 — Summary

- Fieldwork reveals what people actually do, in context
- Ethnography and contextual inquiry put the participant in the role of expert
- Record carefully, watch for the observer effect, triangulate, and get consent
