## Slide 1 — Personas and scenarios

- Week 3 of Human-Computer Interaction
- Two tools for keeping real users in view while designing

## Slide 2 — Why designers lose sight of users

- Designers tend to design for themselves, or for an "elastic user" who stretches to fit whatever feature is being argued for
- Research data (interviews, observation) is rich but hard to carry into every design decision
- We need a compact, shared stand-in for the people we studied

## Slide 3 — What a persona is

- A rich description of an archetypal user, built from research data
- Not a real individual and not an average: a pattern of goals and behaviours seen across several participants
- Given a name, a photo and a context so the team can talk about "what would Priya do?"

## Slide 4 — What personas are for

- The main purpose: to focus design decisions on specific users' goals and to build empathy with them
- They make arguments concrete: "Priya needs this to work on a bus" beats "users might want…"
- They help the team say no to features nobody in the target group needs

## Slide 5 — What goes into a persona

- Goals (what they are trying to achieve), behaviours and context of use
- Frustrations and pain points with current tools
- Relevant skills and attitudes, not irrelevant trivia (favourite colour)
- Every attribute should trace back to the research

## Slide 6 — Primary, secondary and anti-personas

- Primary persona: the main target; the design must satisfy them
- Secondary persona: mostly satisfied by the primary's design, with some additions
- Anti-persona (negative persona): someone the product is deliberately not designed for

## Slide 7 — Risks of personas

- Personas invented without research ("ad hoc" personas) encode the team's assumptions and stereotypes
- Too many personas dilutes focus
- A persona is only useful if the team actually uses it in decisions

## Slide 8 — What a scenario is

- A short narrative of a persona using a product to achieve a goal, in a specific context
- Describes what happens, not how the interface looks
- Problem scenarios describe the current situation; activity scenarios describe the envisioned one

## Slide 9 — Why scenarios help

- They expose requirements and edge cases early ("what if the bus has no signal?")
- They are cheap to write and easy for stakeholders to read and challenge
- They connect personas to tasks, which later become use cases and prototypes

## Slide 10 — Summary

- Personas: research-based archetypes that keep specific users' goals in view
- Scenarios: stories of those personas achieving goals in context
- Both are communication tools for the whole team, and both must be grounded in data
