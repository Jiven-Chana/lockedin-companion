## Slide 1 — Understanding users

- Week 2 of Human-Computer Interaction
- Cognition: how people perceive, attend, remember and reason while using technology

## Slide 2 — Attention

- People can only attend to a little at once; interruptions and multitasking cost performance
- Design implication: make what matters salient and avoid clutter

## Slide 3 — Memory

- Recognition is easier than recall: choosing from a menu beats remembering a command
- The often-quoted "7 ± 2" is about short-term memory span, not a rule for menu length
- Design implication: show options rather than make people remember them

## Slide 4 — Mental models

- A mental model is a user's internal understanding of how a system works
- It is built from experience and is often incomplete or wrong
- Users predict what will happen from their mental model

## Slide 5 — Conceptual models

- The designer's high-level description of how the system is organised and works
- A good design makes its conceptual model visible, so users form an accurate mental model

## Slide 6 — The gulfs of execution and evaluation

- Gulf of execution: the gap between what the user wants to do and the actions the system offers
- Gulf of evaluation: the gap between the system's state and the user's ability to tell what happened
- Good design bridges both: clear actions and clear feedback
